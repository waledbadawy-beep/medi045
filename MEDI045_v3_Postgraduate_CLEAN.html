Station 13 · Crisis Simulation Module

Intraoperative Emergency · CRM · SBAR · Debrief

0% ›
📚 About This Module — Crisis Simulation
This station uses a Standard Simulation Module (SSM) — a structured, reproducible clinical scenario used in high-fidelity simulation training. The case presented (Intraoperative Anaphylaxis during Routine Induction) is a worked example of the SSM format. You are expected to:

① Study the scenario before the session (pre-briefing)
② Perform or observe the simulation in the skill lab
③ Self-assess each performance step below
④ Complete the SBAR handover exercise
⑤ Reflect using the structured debrief questions

Other SSM scenarios you may encounter include: Cannot Intubate Cannot Oxygenate (CICO), Malignant Hyperthermia, Massive Haemorrhage, High Spinal Block, Cardiac Arrest in the OR.
📋 Learning Objectives
1. Recognise the clinical triad of intraoperative anaphylaxis (cardiovascular + respiratory + cutaneous)
2. Execute the first-line and secondary management algorithm including IM adrenaline
3. Demonstrate Crisis Resource Management (CRM): leadership, delegation, closed-loop communication
4. Differentiate anaphylaxis from other causes of intraoperative hypotension and bronchospasm
5. Perform a structured SBAR handover to the ICU team
6. Participate constructively in post-simulation debrief
📖 Scenario Brief – Intraoperative Anaphylaxis
Patient: Jane Doe · 42F · 70kg · MRN 789123
Procedure: Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy (Elective) · ASA II
Hx: HTN (Lisinopril 10mg) · Seasonal allergies · NKDA
Pre-op vitals: BP 138/84 · HR 72 · SpO₂ 98% RA · Mallampati II
Induction: Fentanyl 100mcg IV → Propofol 140mg IV → Rocuronium 50mg IV
T+2 min: HR↑110 · BP↓85/45 · Erythematous rash on chest/neck
T+5 min: BP↓70/40 · HR 125 · SpO₂↓92% · Peak PAP↑45cmH₂O · Urticaria
If no IM Adrenaline: BP↓55/30 → Bradycardia 45 → PEA arrest → CPR
Resolution: IM Adrenaline → BP↑110/60 · HR↓95 · PAP↓25 · SpO₂↑100%
🧠 Anaphylaxis Management Algorithm
🔴 IMMEDIATE (within 60 seconds of recognition):
1. STOP all potential triggers (IV infusions, latex exposure)
2. CALL FOR HELP — announce "This is anaphylaxis, I need help NOW"
3. ADRENALINE 1:1000 — 0.5mg (0.5ml) IM anterolateral thigh (preferred)
⚠️ IM Adrenaline is FIRST LINE — do not delay for IV access
🟡 SECONDARY (within 5 minutes):
4. Increase FiO₂ to 100% · Confirm airway is secured (intubate if not)
5. Aggressive IV fluid bolus: 1–2L crystalloid (LR or NS) rapidly
6. Bronchospasm: Salbutamol 2.5–5mg via nebuliser or ETT / IV MgSO₄
7. IV Hydrocortisone 200mg + IV Chlorphenamine 10mg (second-line)
8. If refractory: Adrenaline infusion (0.1–1 mcg/kg/min) or vasopressin
🟢 IF CARDIAC ARREST (PEA):
→ Start CPR immediately · Follow ACLS PEA algorithm
→ IV Adrenaline 1mg (1:10,000) every 3–5 minutes
→ Consider prolonged resuscitation (anaphylaxis mediators take time to clear)

🫁 Airway, Breathing & Circulation
🫁 1. Airway Patency
Assessment: Ensure the breathing passage is clear, open, and free of obstructions. Listen for stridor, gurgling, snoring. Observe chest rise symmetry. Assess need for airway adjuncts.
None required Oropharyngeal airway (OPA/Guedel) Nasopharyngeal airway (NPA) Head tilt–chin lift Jaw thrust Supplemental O₂ via mask Lateral positioning Suction of secretions

🌬️ 2. Respiratory Status
Assessment: Track breathing depth, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation using pulse oximetry. Note any accessory muscle use, paradoxical breathing, or abnormal breath sounds.
❤️ 3. Circulation & Cardiovascular Status
Assessment: Frequently check blood pressure, heart rate, and heart rhythm via ECG monitors. BP should remain within 20% of pre-operative baseline. Monitor for hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, and arrhythmias.
🧠 Consciousness, Wound & Temperature
🧠 4. Level of Consciousness
Assessment: Evaluate mental status to determine how well the patient is awakening from anaesthesia. Use the AVPU scale and assess orientation, ability to follow commands, and presence of emergence delirium or agitation.
Uneventful recovery Emergence delirium / agitation Prolonged sedation Residual neuromuscular blockade Paradoxical excitation Post-operative cognitive dysfunction (POCD)

🩹 5. Surgical Site & Wound Check
Assessment: Inspect dressings, incisions, and drains for unexpected bleeding or fluid accumulation. Note any soakage, swelling, or dehiscence. Verify drain patency and output.

✗ No
🌡️ 6. Thermoregulation
Assessment: Measure temperature to monitor for and treat hypothermia (<36°C) or hyperthermia (>38°C). Hypothermia increases bleeding risk, prolongs drug metabolism, and causes patient discomfort. Shivering increases O₂ consumption by 400%.
😊 Comfort & Fluid Balance
😊 7. Comfort — Pain & PONV in PACU
Assessment: Gauge subjective pain, nausea, and vomiting using standard patient scales to provide targeted medication. Inadequate pain control in PACU predicts chronic pain development.
Not assessed
💧 8. Fluid Balance in PACU
Assessment: Calculate overall intravenous fluid intake against urinary and drain output. Positive balance may indicate fluid overload; negative balance may indicate ongoing haemorrhage or inadequate resuscitation.
📊 Aldrete Score & Discharge Decision
📊 9. Modified Aldrete Scoring System — PACU Discharge Criteria
Purpose: An objective scoring tool to determine if a patient is safe to be discharged from PACU to the ward or home. Each of the 5 categories is scored 0–2. A score of 9 or 10 out of 10 is generally required before discharge.
Move all 4 extremities = 2 · Move 2 extremities = 1 · Unable to move extremities = 0
Deep breath & cough freely = 2 · Dyspnoeic / limited breathing = 1 · Apnoeic = 0
BP within ±20% of pre-op = 2 · BP within ±20–50% = 1 · BP >50% different from baseline = 0
Fully awake = 2 · Arousable on calling = 1 · Not responding = 0
SpO₂ >92% on room air = 2 · SpO₂ >90% with O₂ = 1 · SpO₂ <90% even with O₂ = 0
